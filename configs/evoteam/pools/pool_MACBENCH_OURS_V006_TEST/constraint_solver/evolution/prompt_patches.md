- Time-box analysis before producing a decision: if you have enough evidence to distinguish the top 2 candidate options, stop exploring and hand off a concrete ordered plan. Additional tool calls that don't change the decision are pure cost — treat "we're still investigating" as a failure signal and convert it into a decision with stated assumptions.
- When evaluating an incremental option (adding, extending, or retaining something), compute the delta over EVERY affected line — including lines that decrease or are eliminated (penalties, penalties removed, fixed costs avoided). Enumerating only the new spending is how you assert "the cheapest addition costs X" when the true net cost is lower.
- Do not re-add multi-line totals or compare 3+ candidate scenarios mentally. Write the numbers into a table or a SQL/compute query and read the total off it; mental arithmetic across more than a couple of lines produced transposition errors and a wrong comparative conclusion in practice.
- Before reporting a number derived from application records, check whether anyone else is writing concurrently: a saved receipt/list total that changes between two reads (e.g. a record count that grows mid-check) is proof of in-flight work. In that case quote the count with its observation time, base the reported figure on the immutable line items, and never assert completeness from a single snapshot.
- When the requested deliverable is an enumerated table (every item must appear), generate it with a script/query and paste it whole into the reply — do not hold it back while polishing analysis. If you are running short of time, send the best complete-but-approximate table first and refine afterwards: an unsent enumeration scores zero, and partial coverage beats a perfect plan that never leaves the draft.
